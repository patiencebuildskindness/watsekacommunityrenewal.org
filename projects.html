Public Art Project

Hubbard Trail: The Path That Built Watseka

A community mural at 203 E. Walnut Street, downtown Watseka

Watseka Community Renewal is bringing a brand-new mural to downtown Watseka, celebrating the rich history and enduring spirit of our community. Centered on the theme “Hubbard Trail: The Path That Built Watseka,” the mural is a vibrant reminder of our shared heritage, and, true to our mission, it is being built by the community. Residents chose the design, helped bring it to life, and helped pay for it. The panels are in their final stretch at the artist’s workshop, and we are ready to unveil them at 203 E. Walnut Street on October 2.

You’re Invited: Mural Ribbon Cutting

Join us and the Watseka Area Chamber of Commerce as we unveil “Hubbard Trail: The Path That Built Watseka” at noon on Friday, October 2, 2026, at the mural site, 203 E. Walnut St. at Walnut and Fourth. Everyone is welcome, admission is free, and the whole community helped make this happen.

How the Mural Is Being Made

Painted panel by panel, by the hands of people who live here.

The Artist Behind the Design

Carol Kaufmann created the winning design and is leading the project’s volunteer artists, guiding the work from first sketch to final panel.

The painting is happening at her workshop, where community volunteers work alongside her, no professional training required. It is exactly how we hoped this mural would come together.

Painted on Aluminum Panels

Rather than paint straight onto brick, the mural is being painted on aluminum panels, which are then mounted at 203 E. Walnut Street.

That choice protects the artwork for the long run. Panels can be maintained and repaired, and if it ever becomes necessary, they can be taken down and moved to another location without losing the mural. It is built to outlast the wall it hangs on.

Thank You for Voting!

The community turned out in a big way, both online and at in-person ballot stations throughout July:

  • Watseka Farmers Market
  • Watseka Public Library
  • Community Meetings, with pop-up voting stations at organizations across town

A total of 1,276 votes were counted, and Design Option 2 came out on top. That design is now taking shape on aluminum panels at the artist’s workshop. Sign up for email updates to hear what WCR takes on next.
